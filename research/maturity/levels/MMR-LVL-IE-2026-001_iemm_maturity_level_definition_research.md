# 1. Purpose

This document investigates how maturity levels for Institution Engineering should be defined.

The purpose is not to impose a predetermined number of levels.

The purpose is to determine whether the historical and methodological evidence reconstructed in MMR-EVMAP-IE-2026-001 supports distinct qualitative maturity states.

The research sequence is:

```text
Historical Evidence

        ↓

        ↓

```

No maturity level shall be defined merely because a historical epoch or transition exists.

# 4. Level Definition Problem

A maturity model requires discrete levels.

Institution Engineering evidence, however, develops continuously and unevenly across multiple dimensions.

Therefore the central research problem is:

> Under what conditions does a qualitative change in demonstrated Institution Engineering capability justify recognition as a distinct maturity level?

The problem cannot be solved by chronology alone.

It also cannot be solved by counting transitions.

A maturity boundary must represent a meaningful change in what the methodology can demonstrably and reliably do.

---

# 5. Working Definition of a Maturity Level

For this research, a maturity level is provisionally defined as:

> A Maturity Level is a qualitatively distinct state of demonstrated methodological capability, bounded by explicit capability gates and supported by sufficient evidence across required maturity dimensions.

A maturity level therefore requires more than improvement.

It requires a defensible state boundary.

Candidate characteristics include:

```text
qualitative distinctness

demonstrated capability

minimum evidence sufficiency

critical-dimension satisfaction

repeatable characteristics where required

verification appropriate to the level

stable separation from the preceding state
```

This definition remains provisional until level-boundary research is complete.

---

# 6. Maturity Level Is Not a Score Band

This research rejects a simple numerical score-band model as the primary level-definition mechanism.

For example:

```text
0–20

Level 1

21–40

Level 2

41–60

Level 3
```

would create boundaries from arithmetic rather than demonstrated capability.

Likewise:

```text
Average Dimension Score

=

Maturity Level
```

is insufficient.

A system may score highly in several dimensions while failing a critical maturity gate.

Therefore maturity levels shall be investigated primarily as capability-and-evidence states rather than numerical intervals.

# 8. Boundary Strength Model

Candidate boundaries require a way to distinguish weak historical change from strong maturity change.

The following working boundary-strength states are introduced:

```text
B0 — NO MATURITY BOUNDARY EVIDENCE

The observed change does not justify a separate maturity state.


B1 — WEAK BOUNDARY CANDIDATE

A qualitative difference may exist,
but evidence or persistence is insufficient.


B2 — MODERATE BOUNDARY CANDIDATE

A meaningful qualitative difference is supported,
but one or more major maturity gates remain unresolved.


B3 — STRONG BOUNDARY CANDIDATE

A distinct capability state is strongly supported
by evidence and relevant maturity gates.


B4 — LEVEL-DEFINING BOUNDARY CANDIDATE

The evidence supports treating the boundary
as a candidate separator between maturity levels.
```

These are research classifications.

They are not maturity levels.

A B4 classification does not itself establish a canonical IEMM level.

It indicates that a transition has sufficient maturity significance to enter candidate level-structure construction.

# 10. Transition Does Not Equal Level Boundary

A major distinction governs the remainder of this research:

```text
Historical Transition

≠

Maturity-Level Boundary
```

A transition may occur:

```text
within a maturity level

between maturity levels

as a precursor to a maturity boundary

as an evidence-strengthening event

as a methodological refinement

without changing maturity level
```

Therefore the research must evaluate each transition separately.

In particular:

```text
T3a

and

T3b
```

shall not automatically become separate maturity boundaries merely because the Evidence Map distinguishes them historically.

Their relationship must be tested for:

```text
independent qualitative distinctness

independent critical bottleneck change

independent evidence floor

independent operational consequence

or

one combined maturity progression
```

Likewise:

```text
T6
```

may represent advanced methodological reflexivity without necessarily constituting a new overall maturity level.

The research must distinguish:

```text
dimension-level advancement

from

overall maturity-level advancement
```

The distinction between transition structure and maturity structure is therefore preserved.

# 27. T6 Boundary Analysis — Explicit Methodological Maturity Assessment

T6 represents the transition:

```text

        ↓

```

Methodological Reflexivity predates the present Maturity Model Research.

Institution Engineering had already demonstrated the ability to apply research reasoning to questions about its own methodology.

T6 therefore does not represent the first appearance of reflexivity.

The new capability is more specific.

Institution Engineering begins explicitly investigating:

```text
What constitutes methodological maturity?

Which dimensions constitute maturity?

What evidence demonstrates maturity?

What qualitative transitions justify levels?

What evidence is insufficient?

What prevents maturity advancement?

How should maturity regression be represented?
```

This is a real meta-methodological advancement.

However, the existence of maturity research does not itself demonstrate that the methodology has crossed a new overall maturity level.

Otherwise the model would risk circular reasoning:

```text
We Study Our Own Maturity

        therefore

We Are More Mature
```

That inference is not permitted.

Explicit maturity assessment must therefore be evaluated as a capability in its own right rather than as automatic evidence of overall level advancement.

# 60. Level Retention, Regression, and Evidence Freshness

Maturity shall not be treated as a permanent entitlement created by historical achievement alone.

A methodology may historically demonstrate a capability and later cease to satisfy the conditions required to claim that capability as current.

Therefore:

```text
Historical Achievement

≠

Permanent Current Maturity
```

The candidate model distinguishes at least three states of level evidence:

```text
CURRENTLY DEMONSTRATED

The required capability and evidence remain
sufficiently current and valid.


HISTORICALLY DEMONSTRATED

The capability was demonstrated historically,
but current validity requires reassessment.


NO LONGER DEMONSTRATED

Required capability, evidence, or critical
gate conditions are no longer satisfied.
```

Candidate retention principle:

```text
A candidate maturity level may be retained
as CURRENT only while its critical capability
gates and required evidence remain valid
under the applicable freshness rules.
```

Candidate regression principle:

```text
If a critical gate required for the current
candidate level is no longer satisfied,
the methodology shall not continue to claim
that level solely because it achieved it
historically.
```

Regression does not erase history.

It changes the current maturity claim.

Therefore:

```text
Historical Record

PRESERVED


Historical Highest Demonstrated Level

PRESERVED


Current Demonstrated Level

REASSESSABLE


Current Level Claim

FAIL-CLOSED ON CRITICAL GATE LOSS
```

The candidate model does not yet prescribe automatic one-level-at-a-time regression.

The reassessed level shall be the highest candidate level whose complete required gates and evidence floor remain satisfied.

For example:

```text
If G34 evidence is no longer sufficient

but G23 remains fully satisfied:

Candidate Current Capability Ceiling

→ IEMM-M3


If G23 critical capability is also lost

but G12 remains fully satisfied:

Candidate Current Capability Ceiling

→ IEMM-M2
```

This is a candidate assessment rule only.

No current Institution Engineering level is assessed in this document.

The interaction between evidence freshness, temporary evidence unavailability, superseded artifacts, invalidated evidence, and true capability regression requires explicit validation before canonical adoption.

# 62. Capability Inheritance and Monotonicity

The candidate level structure is ordinal only because higher candidate levels require preservation of capabilities necessary for lower levels.

The working inheritance rule is:

```text
Candidate IEMM-M4

requires current satisfaction of
M4-specific capability

AND

the capability foundation required for M3

AND

the capability foundation required for M2.


Candidate IEMM-M3

requires current satisfaction of
M3-specific capability

AND

the capability foundation required for M2.
```

This shall be called capability monotonicity.

It does not mean every individual metric must increase numerically.

It means a higher overall level cannot remain valid after losing a lower-level critical capability on which the higher level depends.

Therefore:

```text
Capability Monotonicity

Higher Level Validity

requires

Lower Critical Capability Preservation
```

The following interpretations are rejected:

```text
High Advanced-Dimension Performance

does not compensate for

Loss of Required Lower-Level Governance.


Historical M4 Achievement

does not compensate for

Current Failure of an M2 Critical Gate.


Numerical Average

does not restore

A Failed Critical Bottleneck.
```

This preserves the Critical Bottleneck Principle across the level hierarchy.

# 64. Evidence Freshness and Capability Loss Separation

Evidence freshness and capability regression are related but not identical.

An old artifact does not automatically prove capability loss.

Likewise, historical evidence does not automatically prove current capability.

The candidate model therefore distinguishes:

```text
CASE F1

Evidence is old,
but capability is independently reverified.

Result:

CURRENT CAPABILITY MAY REMAIN DEMONSTRATED.


CASE F2

Evidence is historical,
and current capability has not been reassessed.

Result:

HISTORICALLY DEMONSTRATED /
CURRENT STATUS UNRESOLVED.


CASE F3

Evidence is superseded or invalidated,
but replacement current evidence proves
the same required capability.

Result:

CAPABILITY MAY REMAIN CURRENT.


CASE F4

Required capability is directly shown
to be absent or no longer operational.

Result:

CAPABILITY REGRESSION EVIDENCE.


CASE F5

Required evidence is unavailable,
and current capability cannot be verified.

Result:

FAIL-CLOSED CURRENT LEVEL CLAIM,
WITHOUT AUTOMATIC CLAIM THAT THE
HISTORICAL CAPABILITY NEVER EXISTED.
```

This distinction prevents evidence staleness from being confused with demonstrated capability loss.

It also prevents missing current evidence from being treated as permission to retain a historical level indefinitely.
