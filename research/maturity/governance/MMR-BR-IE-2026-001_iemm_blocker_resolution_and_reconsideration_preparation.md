# 4. Research Method and Evidence Admissibility

Section 4 establishes the methodology governing evidence collection and interpretation within the MMR-BR-IE-2026-001 lifecycle.

This methodology applies before any substantive BR-01 through BR-04 evidence conclusion is made.

Nothing in this section changes a predecessor GAR-S state, GAR-D positive-gate result, or overall adoption-readiness decision.

## 4.1 Evidence First Application

The governing research principle is:

```text
EVIDENCE FIRST
```
For this lifecycle, Evidence First means that no blocker-resolution claim, evidence-sufficiency claim, authority-readiness claim, canonicalization-readiness claim, or change-control-readiness claim may be established solely from expectation, architectural preference, intended future work, or researcher inference.

A research claim must be traceable to admissible evidence appropriate to the type of claim being made.

The lifecycle shall distinguish:

```text
OBSERVED EVIDENCE

DOCUMENTED GOVERNANCE EVIDENCE

INDEPENDENT VALIDATION EVIDENCE

REPOSITORY / ARTIFACT EVIDENCE

EXTERNAL AUTHORITATIVE EVIDENCE

RESEARCH INFERENCE

MISSING EVIDENCE
```

Research inference may organize or interpret admissible evidence.

Research inference is not itself evidence of institutional authority, approval, validation, canonical status, or implementation state.

## 4.2 Admissible Evidence Classes

The following evidence classes are admissible for BR research when relevant to the claim under examination.

### BR-AE1 — Sealed Predecessor Evidence

Evidence contained in or directly traceable to sealed predecessor research artifacts and their verified repository identities.

For the current lifecycle, this includes the sealed MMR-GAR-IE-2026-001 baseline.

### BR-AE2 — Repository and Artifact Evidence

Directly inspectable repository state, canonical documents, versioned artifacts, tests, records, tags, commits, and other reproducible project evidence.

### BR-AE3 — Governance Evidence

Constitutions, charters, adopted standards, approved decisions, authority records, review records, approval records, or other artifacts that can demonstrate governance authority or governance procedure.

A research proposal describing a desired authority structure is not equivalent to evidence that such authority has been granted.

### BR-AE4 — Independent Validation Evidence

Evidence produced through review, evaluation, testing, replication, comparison, or assessment that is sufficiently independent from the originating research claim to provide meaningful validation.

The degree of independence must be recorded rather than assumed.

### BR-AE5 — External Authoritative Evidence

External standards, peer-reviewed research, official institutional publications, recognized methodological references, or other authoritative external sources relevant to the research question.

External authority does not automatically establish internal KOP Labs governance authority.

### BR-AE6 — Research Analysis

Structured comparison, synthesis, inference, gap analysis, or candidate-model reasoning produced within this lifecycle.

Research Analysis is admissible for research interpretation but cannot independently prove approval, adoption, institutional authority, implementation, or independent validation.

### BR-AE7 — Explicit Missing-Evidence Record

A traceable record that required evidence has not been located, does not yet exist, is inaccessible, or has not yet been established.

Explicitly recording missing evidence is preferable to filling the gap through assumption.

## 4.3 Evidence Source Hierarchy

Evidence shall be evaluated according to claim relevance, evidentiary fitness, traceability, and strength rather than by document volume or source prestige alone.

For routing and analysis purposes, the provisional source hierarchy is:

1. SEALED / CANONICAL INTERNAL EVIDENCE
2. DIRECT REPOSITORY OR ARTIFACT EVIDENCE
3. FORMAL GOVERNANCE / AUTHORITY EVIDENCE
4. INDEPENDENT VALIDATION EVIDENCE
5. EXTERNAL AUTHORITATIVE EVIDENCE
6. RESEARCH ANALYSIS
7. UNVERIFIED REPORT OR ASSERTION

This hierarchy is not a universal ranking that makes a numerically higher source class automatically decisive for every research question.

The evidence class must match the claim type.

For example, an external research publication may support a validation method, comparison framework, or methodological requirement, but it cannot prove that KOP Labs has granted a specific internal approval authority.

Likewise, a formal KOP Labs governance decision may prove that a defined authority was granted, but it does not by itself establish independent validation sufficiency for the candidate IEMM.

Repository evidence may directly establish the existence, content, version, or implementation state of an artifact, but repository presence alone does not establish canonical governance status unless competent governance evidence supports that status.

Sealed predecessor evidence controls the historical meaning of predecessor research decisions within its bounded lifecycle state, but it does not automatically establish that later blocker-resolution requirements have been satisfied.

Research Analysis may compare and synthesize evidence from stronger claim-matched sources, but it must not be treated as a substitute for missing authority evidence, independent validation evidence, or direct artifact evidence.

Where admissible evidence classes conflict, the conflict must be preserved for explicit analysis rather than silently resolved by hierarchy alone.

The research record should identify:

- the conflicting evidence items;
- the claim to which the conflict applies;
- the evidence class of each item;
- the relevant lifecycle or temporal state;
- whether one item supersedes another;
- whether the conflict remains unresolved.

Unresolved conflict shall remain visible in the evidence record.

It must not be converted into a positive blocker-resolution claim through researcher preference.

## 4.4 Independence Requirements

Independence is especially material to BR-01 Validation Sufficiency, but independence may also matter wherever a research claim relies on review, challenge, replication, or evaluation outside the originating claim process.

Evidence shall not be classified as independent merely because it appears in a separate file, separate conversation, separate review step, separate model run, or separately named artifact.

Independence analysis shall consider, where applicable:

- whether the evaluator or evaluating process is meaningfully distinct from the originating claimant or claim-generation process;
- whether the evaluation method was independently specified, externally constrained, or meaningfully challengeable;
- whether the evaluation could falsify, reject, downgrade, or materially contradict the originating claim;
- whether the evaluator had access to sufficient underlying evidence to perform the claimed validation;
- whether the evaluation reproduces, tests, compares, audits, or critically reviews the relevant claim;
- whether conflicts of role, authorship, authority, or incentive are disclosed;
- whether the evaluation result is traceable;
- whether the evaluation is reproducible or otherwise reviewable;
- whether the evaluation scope actually covers the claim for which independence is asserted.

The lifecycle may recognize degrees or forms of independence when the evidence supports such differentiation.

However, the lifecycle must not convert internal self-review into independent validation merely by relabeling the review, moving it to another file, repeating it in another session, or assigning it a separate procedural step.

Use of a different AI model, agent, prompt, conversation, or execution session does not by itself establish institutional or methodological independence.

Automation does not by itself establish independence.

Human participation does not by itself establish independence.

External participation does not by itself establish sufficient independence if the evaluator lacks the evidence, method, scope, or freedom necessary to challenge the claim.

For governance questions, independent review and competent approval authority are separate concepts.

Independent review does not grant governance authority.

Governance authority does not by itself establish independent validation.

Where a blocker requires both independent validation and competent governance evidence, both requirements must be evaluated separately and preserved separately in the BR evidence record.

Any future claim that GAR-D03 Validation Sufficiency has become reconsiderable must therefore identify the specific evidence claimed to satisfy the applicable independence requirement rather than relying on the existence of review activity alone.

## 4.5 Temporal and Freshness Treatment

Evidence shall be interpreted relative to the time, artifact version, governance state, and research lifecycle to which it applies.

Each material evidence item should preserve, where available:

- evidence date;
- observation date;
- source or artifact identity;
- artifact version, commit, tag, or equivalent immutable reference;
- applicable lifecycle state;
- applicable governance state;
- supersession status;
- known temporal limitations.

Recent evidence is not automatically stronger than older evidence.

Older evidence is not automatically obsolete.

The controlling question is whether the evidence remains applicable to the bounded claim being evaluated.

Evidence must be treated as potentially stale when:

- the governed artifact has materially changed;
- the validation target has materially changed;
- the relevant authority structure has changed;
- the relevant governance decision has been superseded;
- the applicable standard or procedure has been replaced or materially revised;
- the evidence explicitly applies only to an earlier lifecycle state;
- the evidence depends on conditions that are no longer true.

A later document must not silently rewrite the historical meaning of earlier evidence.

Superseded evidence may remain admissible as historical, lineage, or traceability evidence while no longer being sufficient for a current-state claim.

Sealed predecessor evidence remains authoritative for what the predecessor lifecycle actually established at its sealed state.

It does not automatically prove that the same condition remains true after material changes occurring outside that sealed lifecycle.

Where current-state applicability is uncertain, the evidence record must preserve that uncertainty rather than silently treating historical evidence as current.

Where two evidence items apply to different lifecycle states, the difference in applicable state must be recorded before the items are characterized as conflicting.

Temporal succession alone does not establish semantic supersession.

Supersession must be supported by evidence appropriate to the artifact, rule, decision, or authority relationship involved.

## 4.6 BR Evidence-State Vocabulary

MMR-BR-IE-2026-001 establishes a BR-local evidence-state vocabulary for characterizing evidence available to this research lifecycle.

The states are:

BR-E0 — MISSING

BR-E1 — REPORTED

BR-E2 — PARTIALLY VERIFIED

BR-E3 — VERIFIED

BR-E4 — INDEPENDENTLY VALIDATED

BR-E5 — GOVERNANCE-EVIDENCED

### BR-E0 — MISSING

Required evidence has not been located, does not yet exist, is inaccessible, or has not yet been established.

BR-E0 is an explicit evidence state.

It must not be silently converted into researcher inference.

### BR-E1 — REPORTED

A claim, condition, practice, authority, validation result, or artifact state has been reported or asserted, but sufficient direct verification has not yet been established.

Repeated reporting does not by itself promote BR-E1 to BR-E3.

### BR-E2 — PARTIALLY VERIFIED

Some material aspects of the bounded claim are supported by admissible evidence, while material gaps, uncertainties, dependencies, conflicts, temporal limitations, or unverified elements remain.

BR-E2 must identify what is verified and what remains unresolved.

### BR-E3 — VERIFIED

The bounded research claim is directly supported by sufficient admissible evidence for the specific claim being characterized.

BR-E3 is claim-bounded.

It does not automatically extend to adjacent claims.

BR-E3 does not imply independent validation.

BR-E3 does not imply governance approval.

BR-E3 does not imply canonical adoption.

### BR-E4 — INDEPENDENTLY VALIDATED

The bounded claim has evidence satisfying the applicable independence requirements established by this lifecycle.

BR-E4 requires the claimed independence basis to be traceable.

BR-E4 does not imply governance authority.

BR-E4 does not imply canonical adoption.

### BR-E5 — GOVERNANCE-EVIDENCED

The bounded governance or authority claim is supported by competent governance evidence demonstrating the relevant authority, approval, rule, decision, or governance condition.

BR-E5 applies only to the governance claim supported by that evidence.

BR-E5 does not imply independent validation of unrelated technical, methodological, or empirical claims.

BR-E5 does not itself mean that the candidate IEMM has been canonically adopted.

The BR-E states are evidence characterizations, not a universal linear maturity ladder.

BR-E4 and BR-E5 represent different evidentiary dimensions.

A bounded claim may require BR-E3, BR-E4, BR-E5, or a justified combination depending on the claim type and blocker requirement.

No numerical ordering among BR-E3, BR-E4, and BR-E5 may be used as a substitute for claim-specific sufficiency analysis.

Most importantly, BR-E states are BR research evidence characterizations.

They are not GAR-S states.

They do not modify GAR-S states.

They do not modify GAR-D positive-gate results.

They do not resolve a GAR blocker.

They do not establish positive overall adoption readiness.

They do not authorize canonical IEMM adoption.

They do not authorize current-level assessment.

Any future change to a GAR-S state, GAR-D result, or overall adoption-readiness decision requires a separately authorized GAR reconsideration lifecycle supported by an admissible evidence package.

## 4.7 Conflicting and Missing Evidence Handling

Conflicting evidence and missing evidence are first-class research conditions within MMR-BR-IE-2026-001.

Neither condition may be silently converted into a positive blocker-resolution finding.

When admissible evidence conflicts, the BR research record shall identify, where applicable:

- the bounded claim affected by the conflict;
- each conflicting evidence item;
- the evidence class of each item;
- the source and artifact identity of each item;
- the applicable lifecycle state;
- the applicable temporal state;
- whether either item supersedes the other;
- whether the conflict can be resolved through additional admissible evidence;
- whether the conflict remains unresolved.

A conflict is not resolved merely because one source is newer.

A conflict is not resolved merely because one source is formally styled, longer, more detailed, or more authoritative in an unrelated domain.

A conflict is not resolved merely because one interpretation is preferred by the researcher.

Resolution requires evidence appropriate to the claim and the nature of the conflict.

If the conflict cannot be resolved from admissible evidence, the unresolved conflict must remain explicit.

An unresolved material conflict prevents a claim from being characterized as fully verified for the affected scope unless the unresolved portion is clearly excluded from that bounded claim.

Missing evidence shall be represented explicitly using BR-E0 when the evidence is required for the bounded research question but has not been located or established.

The existence of a research plan, intended future artifact, proposed governance mechanism, expected validation activity, or anticipated approval does not satisfy missing evidence.

A missing-evidence record should identify, where applicable:

- the evidence required;
- the claim or blocker for which it is required;
- why the evidence is material;
- whether the evidence is expected to exist already;
- whether creation of the evidence requires research activity;
- whether creation of the evidence requires governance action;
- whether creation of the evidence requires independent validation;
- whether the evidence is currently inaccessible rather than nonexistent.

Missing evidence must not be replaced by assumption.

Missing evidence must not be promoted from BR-E0 solely because the required artifact has been planned, requested, or proposed.

Where a claim is partially supported but material evidence remains missing, BR-E2 may be appropriate if the verified and missing portions are explicitly distinguished.

## 4.8 Research Evidence and Governance Authority Separation

MMR-BR-IE-2026-001 distinguishes research evidence from governance authority.

Research may identify, analyze, compare, or recommend:

- candidate governance structures;
- candidate approval pathways;
- authority requirements;
- approval criteria;
- canonicalization conditions;
- change-control requirements;
- unresolved authority gaps.

Research activity does not itself create competent governance authority.

A research document does not obtain canonical approval authority merely by describing that authority.

A research finding does not become a governance decision merely because the finding is well-supported.

A BR-E3 VERIFIED research characterization does not establish governance authority.

A BR-E4 INDEPENDENTLY VALIDATED characterization does not establish governance authority.

Governance authority claims require competent governance evidence appropriate to the authority being asserted.

Where such evidence exists, BR-E5 may characterize the bounded governance claim as GOVERNANCE-EVIDENCED.

BR-E5 remains claim-bounded.

Evidence that an authority exists does not automatically prove that:

- the authority has exercised a particular approval;
- the authority has adopted the candidate IEMM;
- the authority has authorized current-level assessment;
- validation requirements have been satisfied;
- canonicalization requirements have been satisfied;
- change-control requirements have been satisfied.

Similarly, absence of governance authority cannot be cured through additional research analysis alone when the missing condition is an actual institutional grant of authority.

BR-02 Authority / Approval Clarity must therefore distinguish at least three different questions:

1. What authority is required?
2. What evidence would demonstrate that the authority exists?
3. Does competent governance evidence currently demonstrate that the authority exists?

The first two questions may be answerable through research.

The third requires actual admissible governance evidence.

MMR-BR-IE-2026-001 must not self-bootstrap the authority needed for future GAR reconsideration, canonical adoption, or current-level assessment.

## 4.9 BR Sufficiency Decision Rule

Evidence sufficiency in this lifecycle is claim-specific and blocker-specific.

No single BR-E state is universally sufficient for every claim.

A BR research conclusion may be considered sufficiently supported only when:

- the bounded claim is explicitly identified;
- the evidence required for that claim is identified;
- the available evidence is admissible for that claim type;
- material evidence gaps are explicitly accounted for;
- material evidence conflicts are explicitly accounted for;
- temporal applicability has been evaluated;
- any required independence condition has been evaluated;
- any required governance-authority condition has been evaluated;
- the evidence characterization is traceable;
- the conclusion does not exceed the scope of the evidence.

Sufficiency must be evaluated against the requirement of the specific blocker or research claim.

For BR-01 Validation Sufficiency, verified evidence may be insufficient when meaningful independent validation is itself a required condition.

For BR-02 Authority / Approval Clarity, research verification may be insufficient when the claim requires competent governance evidence.

For BR-03 Change-Control Readiness, evidence describing a proposed control model may be insufficient if the bounded claim concerns an actually adopted or authorized control mechanism.

For BR-04 Canonicalization Boundary Clarity, a research definition of candidate boundaries may be sufficient for a research-definition claim but insufficient for a claim that those boundaries have been canonically adopted.

A sufficient BR research finding therefore means only that the bounded BR claim has sufficient admissible evidence for the research conclusion being made.

It does not mean that the associated GAR criterion has changed state.

It does not mean that the associated GAR dimension has become positive.

It does not mean that the associated blocker has been resolved.

It does not mean that positive overall adoption readiness has been established.

Where the evidence is insufficient, the research record shall preserve the insufficiency and identify the remaining gap or dependency.

Where the evidence is sufficient only for part of a claim, the conclusion shall remain bounded to that verified part.

BR research shall prefer a narrower supported conclusion over a broader unsupported conclusion.

## 4.10 No Automatic GAR-State Promotion

MMR-BR-IE-2026-001 is a blocker-resolution and reconsideration-preparation research lifecycle.

It is not itself a GAR reconsideration lifecycle.

No evidence characterization, research conclusion, workstream completion, or BR sufficiency finding produced in this lifecycle automatically changes any predecessor GAR state.

In particular:

- BR-E0 through BR-E5 do not map automatically to GAR-S0 through GAR-S5;
- BR-E3 VERIFIED does not automatically promote a GAR criterion;
- BR-E4 INDEPENDENTLY VALIDATED does not automatically promote a GAR criterion;
- BR-E5 GOVERNANCE-EVIDENCED does not automatically promote a GAR criterion;
- completion of a BR workstream does not automatically change a GAR-D positive-gate result;
- accumulation of evidence across multiple BR workstreams does not automatically establish positive overall adoption readiness.

A BR finding may establish that a bounded research requirement has been satisfied for purposes of this lifecycle.

Such a finding is evidence for possible future reconsideration.

It is not the reconsideration decision.

The predecessor GAR-S states remain historical predecessor states until a separately authorized GAR reconsideration lifecycle evaluates admissible new evidence under an explicit reconsideration scope.

The predecessor GAR-D positive-gate results remain unchanged until such a lifecycle is authorized to reconsider them.

A later GAR reconsideration lifecycle may accept, reject, narrow, supplement, or otherwise evaluate BR evidence according to its own authorized decision scope.

MMR-BR-IE-2026-001 therefore has no authority to self-promote its research findings into GAR state changes.

It also has no authority to infer a GAR state change merely because all currently identified BR research gaps appear satisfied.

No automatic promotion path exists from:

to
GAR criterion state
to
GAR dimension result
to
overall adoption readiness.

## 4.11 Future GAR Reconsideration Trigger

A future GAR reconsideration lifecycle may become research-ready only when there is a traceable basis for reconsidering one or more preserved GAR blockers.

For MMR-BR-IE-2026-001, a reconsideration trigger is not a GAR state change.

It is a bounded finding that sufficient new admissible evidence or governance conditions exist to justify opening a separately authorized reconsideration process.

A candidate reconsideration trigger should identify:

- the GAR criterion or criteria proposed for reconsideration;
- the associated GAR dimension or dimensions;
- the predecessor GAR state being preserved;
- the new evidence claimed to justify reconsideration;
- the BR evidence characterization of that evidence;
- the source and artifact identity of the evidence;
- temporal applicability;
- relevant independence evidence;
- relevant governance-authority evidence;
- unresolved conflicts;
- remaining missing evidence;
- dependencies on other BR workstreams;
- the exact scope proposed for reconsideration.

A trigger must be criterion-specific or otherwise explicitly bounded.

Evidence sufficient to reconsider one GAR criterion does not automatically justify reconsideration of another criterion.

Evidence sufficient to reconsider one dimension does not automatically reopen the complete GAR decision.

A workstream may conclude that a reconsideration trigger has not yet been established.

That is a valid research result.

A workstream may also conclude that research requirements are satisfied while a governance dependency remains unresolved.

That does not establish a reconsideration trigger for the governance-dependent claim.

Where a candidate trigger depends on competent authority, the required authority evidence must exist rather than merely be proposed.

Where a candidate trigger depends on independent validation, the applicable independence evidence must be traceable.

Where material evidence remains missing or materially conflicting, the trigger analysis must preserve that condition.

MMR-BR-IE-2026-001 may identify and prepare a candidate reconsideration trigger.

It may not exercise the reconsideration authority that the trigger would support.
