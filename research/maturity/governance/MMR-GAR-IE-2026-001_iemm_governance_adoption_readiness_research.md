# 8. Adoption Readiness Definition

For this research, adoption readiness is provisionally defined as:

> Adoption Readiness is the demonstrated condition in which a research-established candidate maturity model has sufficient governance clarity, evidence support, validation, authority definition, version identity, and change-control discipline to be submitted for a canonical adoption decision without implying that adoption itself has occurred.

Adoption readiness therefore means:

```text
Ready for Adoption Decision

not

Already Adopted
```

The distinction is mandatory.

A candidate model may be:

```text
research-complete

internally coherent

evidence-supported

operationally meaningful
```

and still fail adoption readiness because governance or authority conditions remain unresolved.

Therefore:

```text
Candidate Model Completeness

≠

Adoption Readiness
```

and:

```text
Adoption Readiness

≠

Canonical Adoption
```

Adoption readiness is a decision-preparation state.

It is not an adoption act.

# 11. Criteria Classification Method

Candidate adoption-readiness criteria require role classification before satisfaction assessment.

For this research, criterion roles are:

```text
MANDATORY

A criterion whose failure prevents a positive
adoption-readiness determination.


CONDITIONAL

A criterion that becomes mandatory when its
defined triggering condition applies.


SUPPORTING

A criterion that strengthens readiness evidence
but whose isolated failure does not automatically
block adoption readiness.
```

Classification answers:

```text
What governance role does this criterion play?
```

It does not answer:

```text
Is the criterion currently satisfied?
```

Therefore:

```text
Criterion Classification

≠

Criterion Satisfaction
```

A criterion may be classified as MANDATORY while its present satisfaction state remains unassessed.

Likewise, a SUPPORTING criterion may still expose material evidence relevant to a later adoption decision.

The classification model is fail-closed with respect to mandatory criteria:

```text
MANDATORY CRITERION

+

NOT SATISFIED

or

INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE

=

ADOPTION READINESS CANNOT BE POSITIVE
```

This rule defines governance significance.

It does not determine the present state of any criterion.

# 14. Evidence Requirement Model

A mandatory adoption-readiness criterion cannot be treated as satisfied merely because supporting language exists in a research document.

Criterion satisfaction requires evidence appropriate to the nature of the criterion.

The initial evidence-requirement classes are:

```text
GAR-ER1

DEFINITION EVIDENCE

Evidence that the required concept, rule,
boundary, model, or procedure is explicitly
defined.


GAR-ER2

TRACEABILITY EVIDENCE

Evidence that the criterion is traceable to
identified predecessor research, decisions,
or governed evidence.


GAR-ER3

VALIDATION EVIDENCE

Evidence that the relevant model, boundary,
procedure, or behavior has been independently
tested, challenged, or prospectively validated
where validation is required.


GAR-ER4

AUTHORITY EVIDENCE

Evidence that the responsible approving body,
decision mechanism, or authority path is
explicitly established.


GAR-ER5

IDENTITY EVIDENCE

Evidence that the governed model has a stable,
unambiguous document, version, and canonical
identity.


GAR-ER6

CONTROL EVIDENCE

Evidence that change control, supersession,
revision, rollback, or related governance
controls are explicitly defined.


GAR-ER7

GAP-GOVERNANCE EVIDENCE

Evidence that unresolved uncertainty is
explicitly preserved, classified, and governed
rather than silently treated as resolved.


GAR-ER8

SEPARATION EVIDENCE

Evidence that adoption, assessment, level
assignment, and related authorities remain
structurally distinguishable.
```

Evidence classes are not interchangeable.

For example:

```text
DEFINITION EVIDENCE

does not automatically constitute

VALIDATION EVIDENCE
```

Likewise:

```text
REPOSITORY PRESENCE

does not automatically constitute

AUTHORITY EVIDENCE
```

The evidence requirement for a criterion may contain one or more evidence classes.

A criterion shall not be marked satisfied until its required evidence classes are sufficiently supported.

# 15. Criterion Satisfaction-State Model

Criterion satisfaction requires explicit states.

The candidate state model is:

```text
GAR-S0

NOT ASSESSED

No criterion-level satisfaction determination
has yet been performed.


GAR-S1

INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE

The criterion has been examined,

but the available evidence is insufficient
to determine satisfaction.


GAR-S2

NOT SATISFIED

Sufficient evidence exists to determine
that the criterion is not currently met.


GAR-S3

PARTIALLY SATISFIED

The criterion has meaningful supporting evidence,

but one or more required elements remain
unsatisfied or insufficiently supported.


GAR-S4

SATISFIED

All required elements and required evidence
classes for the criterion are sufficiently
supported.


GAR-S5

SATISFIED WITH PRESERVED CONDITION

The criterion is sufficiently satisfied
for adoption-readiness purposes,

but an explicitly preserved condition,
dependency, or bounded uncertainty remains
and must continue to be governed.
```

These states distinguish lack of evidence from negative evidence.

Therefore:

```text
INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE

≠

NOT SATISFIED
```

and:

```text
PARTIALLY SATISFIED

≠

SATISFIED
```

A criterion classified as MANDATORY may support a positive readiness result only when its admissible satisfaction state meets the later-defined gate rule.

No criterion state is assigned in this section.

The adoption-readiness model is fail-closed.

For a mandatory criterion:

```text

→

GAR-S2

→

GAR-S3
PARTIALLY SATISFIED

→
POSITIVE READINESS NOT PERMITTED
UNLESS A LATER GOVERNANCE RULE
EXPLICITLY AUTHORIZES CONDITIONAL TREATMENT


GAR-S4
SATISFIED

→
ELIGIBLE TO SUPPORT POSITIVE READINESS


GAR-S5
SATISFIED WITH PRESERVED CONDITION

→
ELIGIBILITY DEPENDS ON THE GOVERNANCE
DISPOSITION OF THE PRESERVED CONDITION
```

Because GAR-C01 through GAR-C20 are currently classified as MANDATORY:

```text
POSITIVE ADOPTION READINESS

requires

ALL MANDATORY CRITERIA

to satisfy the applicable evidence
and gate requirements.
```

This does not mean every preserved uncertainty must disappear.

Instead:

```text
Uncertainty may remain

only if

its governance disposition permits it

and

the affected mandatory criterion remains
sufficiently satisfied.
```

Dimension-level readiness shall not be calculated by arithmetic averaging.

A universal adoption gate fails if any mandatory criterion assigned to that dimension fails its applicable gate requirement.

Therefore:

```text
HIGH PERFORMANCE IN OTHER DIMENSIONS

cannot compensate for

FAILURE OF A UNIVERSAL ADOPTION GATE
```

No dimension readiness state is assigned here.

No overall adoption-readiness determination is made here.

# 17. Evidence-Class Mapping Method

Each mandatory adoption-readiness criterion requires explicit evidence classes before satisfaction may be assessed.

The mapping answers:

```text
What kinds of evidence must exist

before this criterion may be evaluated
as satisfied?
```

It does not answer:

```text
Does the required evidence currently exist?
```

and it does not answer:

```text
What is the current GAR-S state?
```

Therefore:

```text
Evidence-Class Requirement Mapping

≠

Evidence Collection

≠

Evidence Sufficiency Assessment

≠

Criterion Satisfaction Assessment
```

The mapping uses two requirement strengths:

```text
REQUIRED

The evidence class must be sufficiently supported
for the criterion to reach an admissible positive
satisfaction state.


CONDITIONAL

The evidence class becomes required when the
criterion's governed condition activates the
need for that evidence.
```

No OPTIONAL evidence class is introduced at this stage.

Supporting evidence may exist outside the formal requirement set, but it does not substitute for a REQUIRED evidence class.

# 23. Source Verification State Model

Candidate evidence sources require an explicit verification-state model before source content is used in criterion-level evidence sufficiency decisions.

The candidate source-verification states are:

```text
GAR-SV0

NOT INSPECTED

The candidate source has been identified,

but its relevant content has not yet been
inspected for the required evidence class.


GAR-SV1

SOURCE LOCATED

The source identity and relevant candidate
location have been confirmed,

but substantive support has not yet been
verified.


GAR-SV2

PARTIALLY SUPPORTING

The inspected source contains relevant
supporting content,

but the support is incomplete, bounded,
ambiguous, or insufficient to constitute
verified support for the required evidence
claim.


GAR-SV3

VERIFIED SUPPORT

The inspected source contains content that
directly and reproducibly supports the
bounded evidence claim being evaluated.


GAR-SV4

VERIFIED CONTRADICTION

The inspected source contains content that
directly contradicts the bounded evidence
claim being evaluated.


GAR-SV5

REQUIRED EVIDENCE MISSING

The source has been inspected for the
bounded evidence claim,

and the evidence required from that source
is not present.
```

These states operate at the source-claim level.

They do not directly assign a criterion satisfaction state.

Therefore:

```text
GAR-SV State

≠

GAR-S State
```

A source may provide GAR-SV3 VERIFIED SUPPORT for one evidence claim while providing GAR-SV2, GAR-SV4, or GAR-SV5 for another claim.

Verification state shall therefore be assigned to a bounded relationship:

```text
Source

+

Criterion

+

Required Evidence Class

+

Evidence Claim
```

rather than to an entire document globally.

This prevents a source from being treated as universally verified merely because one portion of it supports one requirement.

# 24. Source Content Verification Method

Source verification shall proceed through a fixed Evidence First sequence.

The verification unit is:

```text
Verification Unit

=

Source Identity

+

Criterion ID

+

Required Evidence Class

+

Bounded Evidence Claim
```

For each verification unit, the research shall capture:

```text
SOURCE ID

SOURCE DOCUMENT ID

SOURCE SHA256

CRITERION ID

REQUIRED EVIDENCE CLASS

EVIDENCE CLAIM

SOURCE LOCATION

RELEVANT SOURCE CONTENT

SOURCE FIDELITY

EVIDENCE FRESHNESS

VERIFICATION RESULT

GAR-SV STATE

LIMITATION / GAP

NOTES
```

The minimum verification procedure is:

```text
Step 1

Confirm source identity and SHA256.


Step 2

Confirm that the criterion requires the
evidence class being inspected.


Step 3

Define the bounded evidence claim.


Step 4

Locate the relevant source content.


Step 5

Evaluate source fidelity and applicability.


Step 6

Evaluate whether the content supports,
partially supports, contradicts,
or fails to provide the bounded claim.


Step 7

Assign a GAR-SV state.


Step 8

Record limitations, unresolved gaps,
and any additional evidence requirement.
```

Source verification shall preserve the distinction:

```text
SOURCE VERIFIED

≠

EVIDENCE SUFFICIENT

≠

CRITERION SATISFIED
```

GAR-SV3 VERIFIED SUPPORT means only that a bounded source-level claim is supported.

It does not mean that all required evidence classes for the criterion have been sufficiently supported.

Likewise:

```text
multiple GAR-SV3 observations

do not automatically imply

GAR-S4 SATISFIED
```

Criterion-level sufficiency requires a separate aggregation decision after all required evidence classes have been evaluated.

The current GAR artifact is subject to an additional self-evidence restriction:

```text
GAR-SRC-05

may support

GAR-ER1
DEFINITION EVIDENCE

GAR-ER5
IDENTITY EVIDENCE

GAR-ER7
GAP-GOVERNANCE EVIDENCE

GAR-ER8
SEPARATION EVIDENCE

where directly applicable,

but it shall not be treated as independent
validation evidence or external adoption
authority merely because it defines the
GAR framework itself.
```

Therefore GAR-SRC-05 cannot bootstrap its own governance authority.

The source verification sequence shall begin with sealed predecessor sources before relying on current-artifact self-evidence where independence matters.

No GAR-SV state is assigned in this section.
