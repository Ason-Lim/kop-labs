# 1. Research Purpose

The purpose of Maturity Model Research is not merely to construct a sequence such as M0 → M1 → M2.

The central research question is:

> At what maturity state are Institution Engineering Methodology and Commerce AI Generator currently operating, and what evidence is required to demonstrate transition to the next qualitatively different state?

The intended operating model is:

```text
Current State Assessment
        ↓
Next Maturity Gate
        ↓
Required Evidence / Capability
        ↓
Roadmap
        ↓
Effort / Time Forecast
        ↓
Actual Result
        ↓
Forecast Calibration
```

---

# 2. Definition of Maturity

For this research, maturity is provisionally defined as:

> Maturity is the demonstrated capacity of a methodology or system to repeatedly achieve a defined class of outcomes under increasingly demanding conditions, supported by reproducible evidence.

The key term is:

```text
DEMONSTRATED
```

Therefore:

```text
Designed
≠ Mature

Implemented
≠ Mature

Tested Once
≠ Mature

Succeeded Once
≠ Mature
```

Maturity should consider at least:

```text
Capability
+
Repeatability
+
Evidence
+
Governance
+
Verification
+
Operational Use
```

---

# 3. Independent Maturity Models

Two maturity models shall be researched independently.

## 3.1 IEMM

**Institution Engineering Methodology Maturity Model**

Research question:

> How mature is Institution Engineering as a methodology for researching, designing, governing, implementing, verifying, learning from, and evolving institutions?

## 3.2 CAIMM

**Commerce AI Maturity Model**

Research question:

> At what level of commerce intelligence can Commerce AI Generator reliably solve increasingly complex classes of user and commerce problems?

The two models may influence each other but shall not be assumed to advance synchronously.

```text
IEMM Level
≠
CAIMM Level
```

---

# 4. Dimension and Level Separation

Maturity shall not initially be represented by a single number alone.

A distinction shall be maintained between:

```text
Dimension
= What capability or property is being assessed?

Level
= What overall qualitative maturity state is supported
  by evidence across the required dimensions?
```

A system may therefore have uneven maturity across different dimensions.

# 7. Evidence State

Capability existence and maturity evidence shall be evaluated separately.

Initial Evidence State candidates:

```text
E0 — CLAIMED
The capability is asserted in discussion or documentation.

E1 — OBSERVED
Implementation or an artifact has been directly observed.

E2 — VERIFIED
Explicit verification evidence exists.

E3 — REPEATED
The capability has been repeatedly verified across executions
or relevant cases.

E4 — INDEPENDENTLY VERIFIED
Verification has been performed through an independent
verification boundary.

E5 — OPERATIONALLY VALIDATED
The capability has been validated under relevant operational
conditions.
```

Evidence State is not itself a Maturity Level.

Example:

```text
Capability:
Purchase Route

Capability State:
IMPLEMENTED

Evidence State:
E1
```

This alone shall not justify a high maturity classification.

# 8. Gate-Based Promotion

Maturity Level promotion shall be evidence-gated.

```text
Current Level
      ↓
Required Gate
      ↓
Evidence Satisfied?
   ┌──────┴──────┐
   NO            YES
   │              │
Remain         Promote
```

Each future Level should therefore define at least:

```text
Definition

Entry Condition

Required Capabilities

Required Evidence

Verification Requirement

Exit Gate
```

Elapsed time alone shall not promote maturity.

---

# 9. Discrete Level and Continuous Progress

The research adopts the provisional principle:

> Level is discrete; progress is continuous.

Rather than prematurely declaring fractional official levels such as:

```text
M2.8
```

assessment should preferably use:

```text
Current Official Level:
M2

M3 Gate Completion:
72%

Assessment Confidence:
MEDIUM
```

until the model is validated.

---

# 10. Assessment Confidence

Maturity assessment shall distinguish the maturity conclusion from confidence in that conclusion.

Initial confidence candidates:

```text
HIGH
MEDIUM
LOW
INSUFFICIENT
```

Example:

```text
Current Level:
M2

Next-Level Gate Completion:
68%

Assessment Confidence:
MEDIUM
```

---

# 11. Time Is Not Maturity

Elapsed research or development time is not a maturity criterion.

```text
6 months of research
≠ M3

1 year of development
≠ M4
```

Time is a forecasting variable.

Maturity is an evidence-based assessment.

---

# 12. Effort Forecast

For each future maturity transition, estimated effort should consider:

```text
Remaining Research
+
Implementation
+
Verification
+
Documentation
+
External Dependencies
+
Contingency
─────────────────────
Expected Effort Hours
```

The user's planning baseline for future forecasting is approximately:

```text
5 collaborative research/development hours per active day
```

Effort hours and calendar duration shall remain distinct.

---

# 13. Forecast Ranges

Forecasts should not rely on a single date.

Each maturity transition should eventually estimate:

```text
Earliest
Expected
Conservative
```

Example:

```text
Earliest:
350 hours

Expected:
500 hours

Conservative:
750 hours
```

At five active hours per day:

```text
70 / 100 / 150 active days
```

Actual results should later be compared against forecasts.

# 14. External Research Application

External papers, benchmarks, industry cases, and reported results may inform research findings and architecture or methodology candidates.

They shall not be treated as evidence of local project performance.

The adoption sequence is:

```text
Research Finding
        ↓
Architecture / Method Candidate
        ↓
Project Baseline
        ↓
Controlled Experiment
        ↓
Regression Test
        ↓
Shadow Evaluation
        ↓
Evidence Review
        ↓
Adoption Decision
```

External evidence does not substitute for project-local validation.

---

# 15. Maturity Anti-Patterns

The research shall explicitly avoid the following:

```text
Document Count Maturity
"Many documents therefore mature."

Feature Count Maturity
"Many features therefore mature."

Time Served Maturity
"Long development therefore mature."

Technology Maturity
"Uses newer AI therefore mature."

Self-Declared Maturity
"Declared M4 therefore M4."

Single-Success Maturity
"Succeeded once therefore repeatable."
```
