# 2. Architecture Need

Commerce AI Generator requires an evidence-backed architecture capable of representing, evaluating, comparing, and explaining complete acquisition alternatives across:

```text
marketplaces
countries
currencies
fulfillment models
international logistics
regulatory constraints
customs
taxation
final landed cost
```

while preserving the established authority of existing Commerce AI architecture.

The architecture must support questions such as:

> What product should this user acquire, where should it be purchased, through which route can it legally and practically reach the destination, and what is the estimated final acquisition cost?

---

# 3. Selected Architecture

The selected research-preferred architecture is:

```text
Alternative C
Hybrid Commerce Intelligence
```

The architecture deliberately separates three classes of responsibility:

```text
1. Existing Commerce Architecture

2. Shared Commerce Capability Candidates

3. Cross-Border-Specific Intelligence
```

This avoids both:

```text
a monolithic Cross-Border subsystem
```

and:

```text
premature decomposition into many independent platform services
```

---

# 4. Governing Architecture Thesis

The proposed architecture follows this thesis:

> Cross-Border Commerce should extend Commerce AI through a bounded international acquisition-evaluation capability that orchestrates evidence from existing and specialized commerce capabilities, derives valid Purchase Route Candidates, and passes those candidates to the existing Recommendation Engine for user-specific ranking.

The Cross-Border architecture does not replace Marketplace, Product Identity, Market Intelligence, Recommendation, Experience, or Food Intelligence.

# 6. Existing Architecture Authorities

The following responsibilities remain authoritative and are not reassigned.

## 6.1 Marketplace Core

Responsible for:

```text
marketplace observation
provider normalization
seller/listing representation
marketplace-facing adapters
```

Cross-Border Commerce shall consume canonical marketplace observations.

It shall not own Amazon, Shopee, Rakuten, Shopify, or other marketplace-provider logic directly.

---

## 6.2 Product Identity

Responsible for:

```text
canonical product identity
same-product resolution
variant relationships
equivalence relationships
```

Cross-Border Commerce may require international extensions to Product Identity but shall not duplicate product-matching logic.

---

## 6.3 Food / Product Intelligence

Responsible for understanding product-domain characteristics.

For food:

```text
"What is this product?"
```

remains the responsibility of Food Intelligence.

Cross-Border Commerce is responsible for:

```text
"Can this product be acquired
through this international route?"
```

These responsibilities must remain distinct.

---

## 6.4 Market Intelligence

Responsible for interpretation of market signals.

Cross-Border Commerce may contribute new international observations but must not absorb the established Market Intelligence authority.

---

## 6.5 Recommendation Engine

Responsible for:

```text
ranking
selection
priority
preference application
exploration
trade-off selection
```

Recommendation shall not own:

```text
FX retrieval
customs rules
regulatory rules
shipping quotations
marketplace APIs
```

---

## 6.6 Experience Architecture

Responsible for:

```text
comparison presentation
explanation
interaction
stateful experience
revisit experience
```

Cross-Border Commerce provides structured evidence and candidates to Experience.

It does not create a parallel Cross-Border UI architecture.

# 7. Cross-Border Commerce Intelligence Authority

The proposed bounded Cross-Border responsibility is:

> Evaluate whether and how a product can be acquired across national and marketplace boundaries and produce evidence-backed Purchase Route Candidates for Recommendation.

Its candidate responsibilities are:

```text
Cross-Border Acquisition Context

Regulatory Eligibility

Customs / Tax Evaluation

International Logistics Evaluation

Cross-Border Landed Cost

Purchase Route Composition

Purchase Route Evaluation
```

---

# 8. Cross-Border Acquisition Context

The architecture requires acquisition-specific context such as:

```text
product origin
seller market
marketplace region
shipping origin
destination country
destination region
customs jurisdiction
```

This is distinct from generic:

```text
locale
display language
display currency
user market
```

which may belong to broader Commerce context.

The proposal therefore avoids introducing one ambiguous global `country` field.

---

# 9. Regulatory Eligibility

Regulatory eligibility is a mandatory Cross-Border responsibility.

Candidate states:

```text
ALLOWED

ALLOWED_WITH_CONDITIONS

RESTRICTED

PROHIBITED

UNKNOWN
```

The system must support `UNKNOWN` as a valid result.

Uncertain regulation must not be silently converted into permission.

---

# 10. Regulatory Gate

The proposal establishes:

> Legal and regulatory feasibility must be represented before an acquisition route can be treated as a normal recommendation candidate.

Logical model:

```text
Product / Listing
      ↓
Candidate Route
      ↓
Regulatory Evaluation
      ↓
Allowed / Conditional /
Restricted / Prohibited / Unknown
      ↓
Cost Evaluation
      ↓
Recommendation
```

A low-cost route cannot be considered superior merely because its economic score is favorable.

---

# 11. Customs and Tax Evaluation

# 27. Privacy Principle

> Commerce AI should own commerce intelligence, not unnecessary personal identity data.

Therefore identity, notification endpoints, payment data, shipping information and transaction-specific personal information should remain separated where feasible.

# 39. Architecture Principles

The proposed architecture is governed by:

### CBC-P-001 — Reuse Before Reinvention

Existing canonical architecture retains authority.

### CBC-P-002 — Provider Neutrality

No external provider becomes the core architecture.

### CBC-P-003 — Evidence-Backed Facts

Volatile facts require provenance and freshness.

### CBC-P-004 — Recommendation Does Not Own Facts

Recommendation ranks evidence-backed candidates.

### CBC-P-005 — Legal Feasibility Before Optimization

A prohibited route cannot become best merely because it is cheap.

### CBC-P-006 — Complete Acquisition Cost

Listing price is not equivalent to final user cost.

### CBC-P-007 — Privacy Minimization

Avoid unnecessary personal-data ownership.

### CBC-P-008 — Shared Capabilities Stay Shared

Discovery in Cross-Border research does not imply Cross-Border ownership.

### CBC-P-009 — Cart Preserves Intent

Volatile acquisition facts should be refreshed rather than permanently embedded as Cart truth.

### CBC-P-010 — Purchase Route Is Derived

Purchase Route is a decision candidate built from evidence, not the authoritative owner of all facts.

### CBC-P-011 — Unknown Is Valid

Architecture must preserve uncertainty.

### CBC-P-012 — Extract From Evidence

Shared/platform capability extraction should occur when operational evidence justifies it.
