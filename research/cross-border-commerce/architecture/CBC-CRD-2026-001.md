# 3. Decomposition Principle

The Cross-Border Commerce architecture SHALL follow:

```text
Reuse Existing Canonical Authority
        +
Own Only Cross-Border-Specific Responsibility
        +
Preserve Evidence Provenance
        +
Preserve Unknown State
        +
Avoid Transaction Execution
```

Cross-Border Intelligence SHALL NOT absorb existing responsibilities
merely because they participate in a cross-border recommendation flow.

---

# 4. Canonical Responsibility Model

The initial canonical responsibility model contains six architecture
responsibilities:

```text
1. Cross-Border Intelligence Core

2. Landed-Cost Intelligence

3. Shipping-Route Intelligence

4. Regulatory Evidence and Regulatory Gate

5. Cross-Market Product Comparison

6. Cross-Border Evidence Provenance
```

These are architecture responsibilities.

They are not yet implementation modules, services, packages, APIs,
databases, or deployment units.

---

# 5. Cross-Border Intelligence Core

## 5.1 Owns

Cross-Border Intelligence Core owns coordination of cross-border-specific
decision context.

It owns:

```text
destination-country context
origin-country context
cross-border evaluation context
cross-border evidence aggregation context
cross-border eligibility state
cross-border route candidate context
cross-border comparison orchestration semantics
```

It may coordinate results from other canonical authorities.

## 5.2 Reuses

It reuses:

```text
Recommendation Engine
Marketplace Core
Market Intelligence
Product Intelligence
Experience Architecture
Preference
Session Context
Food Knowledge
```

where those authorities already own the underlying semantics.

## 5.3 Depends On

It may depend on:

```text
Landed-Cost Intelligence
Shipping-Route Intelligence
Regulatory Evidence
Cross-Market Product Comparison
Cross-Border Evidence Provenance
```

## 5.4 Must Not Own

It must not own:

```text
general recommendation scoring
general marketplace normalization
general product identity
general food knowledge
general user preference
general session state
general market intelligence
payment execution
purchase execution
customs brokerage execution
fulfillment execution
```

---

# 6. Landed-Cost Intelligence

## 6.1 Owns

Landed-Cost Intelligence owns cross-border delivered-cost reasoning.

Its canonical responsibility may include:

```text
item price evidence
international shipping cost evidence
forwarding cost evidence
estimated duty
estimated tax
known service fees
known route fees
landed-cost composition
landed-cost completeness state
landed-cost confidence / evidence state
```

## 6.2 Output Principle

A landed-cost result must preserve the difference between:

```text
known
estimated
derived
unknown
unavailable
not applicable
```

## 6.3 Must Not Own

Landed-Cost Intelligence must not own:

```text
seller product price authority
general ranking policy
payment calculation
invoice generation
tax filing
customs declaration
transaction settlement
```

## 6.4 No False Precision Rule

Missing cost components SHALL NOT default to zero unless zero is
supported by authoritative evidence.

Therefore:

```text
unknown duty != zero duty
unknown tax != zero tax
unknown shipping != free shipping
```

---

# 7. Shipping-Route Intelligence

## 7.1 Owns

Shipping-Route Intelligence owns intelligence about feasible
cross-border delivery routes.

Potential route concepts include:

```text
direct international shipping
forwarding-service route
domestic-to-forwarder route
forwarder-to-destination route
carrier route
multi-leg route
route availability
route cost evidence
route timing evidence
route constraint evidence
```

## 7.2 Reuses

It may consume marketplace shipping evidence and external logistics
evidence.

## 7.3 Must Not Own

It must not own:

```text
shipment booking
warehouse execution
carrier dispatch
package consolidation execution
customs brokerage execution
delivery execution
```

Those capabilities remain outside the current architecture scope.

---

# 8. Regulatory Evidence and Regulatory Gate

## 8.1 Owns

Regulatory Evidence responsibility owns the representation of
cross-border regulatory evidence relevant to recommendation safety and
eligibility.

Potential evidence includes:

```text
destination-country restrictions
restricted-item evidence
import eligibility
customs evidence
duty-rule evidence
tax-rule evidence
quantity restrictions
category restrictions
documentation requirements
source authority
effective date
freshness
jurisdiction
```

## 8.2 Regulatory Gate

The Regulatory Gate determines whether available evidence is sufficient
to permit a cross-border recommendation claim.

Possible architecture states must include:

```text
eligible
ineligible
conditional
unknown
insufficient evidence
not applicable
```

## 8.3 Must Not Own

The Regulatory Gate must not own:

```text
legal advice
customs filing
regulatory filing
brokerage execution
government decision authority
```

The system represents and evaluates evidence.

It does not become the regulator.

---

# 9. Cross-Market Product Comparison

## 9.1 Owns

Cross-Market Product Comparison owns cross-border comparison semantics
between marketplace candidates after product identity evidence has been
resolved.

It may compare:

```text
same-product candidates
equivalent-product candidates
substitute-product candidates
local-market offers
overseas-market offers
Korean-market offers
landed-cost results
availability evidence
quality / value / trust evidence
```

## 9.2 Product Identity Boundary

Cross-Market Product Comparison does not automatically own canonical
product identity.

Product identity must remain with the appropriate existing or future
canonical Product Identity authority.

Therefore:

```text
Identity Resolution
        ->
Cross-Market Comparison
```

not:

```text
Cross-Market Comparison
        ->
Invent Product Identity
```

## 9.3 Recommendation Boundary

Cross-Market Product Comparison supplies structured comparison evidence.

Final recommendation policy remains with the canonical Recommendation
Engine unless a later architecture decision explicitly changes that
ownership.

---

# 10. Cross-Border Evidence Provenance

## 10.1 Owns

Cross-Border Evidence Provenance owns the cross-border-specific
provenance requirements needed to preserve evidence traceability.

At minimum, evidence should be able to preserve:

```text
source identity
source type
observation time
effective time
destination jurisdiction
origin jurisdiction
freshness
confidence
derivation status
estimate status
authority class
unknown state
```

## 10.2 Evidence Classification

Evidence should support distinctions such as:

```text
OBSERVED
DERIVED
ESTIMATED
AUTHORITATIVE
NON-AUTHORITATIVE
UNKNOWN
UNAVAILABLE
STALE
```

The final canonical vocabulary remains subject to later architecture
review.

## 10.3 Must Not Own

Evidence Provenance does not own the business meaning of every evidence
item.

It owns provenance semantics required to understand the reliability and
origin of the evidence.

# 11. Existing Recommendation Engine Boundary

The canonical Recommendation Engine remains responsible for general
recommendation semantics.

Cross-Border Intelligence may provide additional structured evidence:

```text
destination context
regulatory state
landed cost
shipping route
cross-market comparison
cross-border provenance
```

The Recommendation Engine may use those inputs without transferring its
general ranking ownership to the Cross-Border domain.

Therefore:

```text
Cross-Border Intelligence
        ->
Canonical Recommendation Evidence

Recommendation Engine
        ->
Final Recommendation Policy
```

---

# 12. Marketplace Core Boundary

Marketplace Core remains authoritative for general marketplace-level
normalization, aggregation, collection boundaries, and marketplace
representation where already established.

Cross-Border Intelligence may consume marketplace evidence but SHALL NOT
duplicate Marketplace Core semantics.

Cross-border-specific interpretation belongs to the Cross-Border
architecture only when the responsibility is genuinely cross-border.

---

# 13. Market Intelligence Boundary

Market Intelligence remains authoritative for general market signals
and market-level intelligence.

Cross-Border Commerce may consume market intelligence when comparing
markets, but SHALL NOT redefine general market intelligence ownership.

A later architecture artifact may define cross-border market-difference
signals if a distinct canonical responsibility is justified.

---

# 14. Product Intelligence Boundary

Product Intelligence remains authoritative for product-level knowledge
where already established.

Cross-Border Commerce may consume:

```text
product attributes
quality evidence
product-category knowledge
product identity evidence
product suitability evidence
```

but SHALL NOT duplicate those responsibilities.

---

# 15. Experience Architecture Boundary

Experience Architecture remains responsible for canonical user
experience context and presentation-oriented experience semantics where
already established.

Cross-Border Intelligence may provide:

```text
destination-aware result context
cost completeness indicators
regulatory uncertainty indicators
route availability indicators
evidence confidence indicators
```

Experience Architecture determines how approved experience semantics are
presented to users.

Cross-Border Intelligence SHALL NOT absorb general experience ownership.

---

# 16. Preference Boundary

Preference remains authoritative for persistent user preference
semantics.

Cross-Border Commerce may consume preference signals such as:

```text
price sensitivity
quality preference
trust preference
delivery preference
market preference
```

if those signals are canonically supported.

Cross-Border Commerce SHALL NOT create a parallel user-preference model
without separate architecture authorization.

---

# 17. Session Context Boundary

Session Context remains authoritative for temporary session-level state.

Cross-Border Commerce may consume session information such as:

```text
current destination
current query
current comparison context
temporary filters
```

where those values are canonically represented.

Cross-Border Intelligence SHALL NOT create competing session ownership.

---

# 18. Food Knowledge Boundary

Food Knowledge remains authoritative for food-domain knowledge.

Cross-Border Commerce may consume food knowledge when evaluating
cross-border food products.

Food Knowledge SHALL NOT be moved into Cross-Border Intelligence.

Regulatory restrictions concerning food importation remain a
cross-border regulatory concern, while intrinsic food knowledge remains
with Food Knowledge.

# 20. Unknown-State Architecture

Unknown-state preservation is mandatory across all six responsibilities.

At minimum, the architecture must distinguish:

```text
KNOWN
ESTIMATED
DERIVED
UNKNOWN
UNAVAILABLE
NOT_APPLICABLE
```

No architecture component may silently convert unknown into a known
default for convenience.

---

# 21. Evidence-First Rule

All cross-border conclusions must remain traceable to evidence or an
explicit derivation.

The architecture should support the conceptual chain:

```text
Source Evidence
        ->
Normalized Evidence
        ->
Provenance
        ->
Cross-Border Interpretation
        ->
Comparison / Eligibility / Cost
        ->
Recommendation Evidence
```

Derived results must remain distinguishable from observed facts.

---

# 22. Regulatory Safety Rule

If regulatory evidence is insufficient, the architecture must be able
to return:

```text
UNKNOWN
INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE
CONDITIONAL
```

rather than incorrectly producing:

```text
ELIGIBLE
```

This rule is architecture-level and binding.

---

# 23. Landed-Cost Completeness Rule

A landed-cost result must carry sufficient state to determine whether
the cost is:

```text
COMPLETE
PARTIAL
ESTIMATED
UNKNOWN
```

A numeric result alone is insufficient if material cost components are
unknown.

---

# 24. Cross-Market Comparison Rule

Cross-market comparison must not compare candidates as if they are
equivalent unless identity or substitution evidence supports that
relationship.

Possible comparison relationships may include:

```text
SAME_PRODUCT
EQUIVALENT
SUBSTITUTE
RELATED
UNKNOWN_RELATIONSHIP
```

The final canonical vocabulary requires subsequent architecture review.

---

# 25. Destination Awareness Rule

Cross-border intelligence must be destination-aware.

The same product or route may produce different outcomes depending on:

```text
destination country
destination region
regulatory jurisdiction
shipping availability
tax / duty applicability
market availability
```

Destination context is therefore part of the canonical cross-border
evaluation context.
