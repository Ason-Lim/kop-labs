# 4. Architecture Classification Review

The governing architecture classification remains:

```text
N2 — Cross-Domain Architecture Extension
```

The classification remains appropriate because the proposed
architecture extends existing commerce intelligence capabilities
across multiple canonical responsibility domains rather than
introducing an isolated implementation feature.

The architecture requires coordinated responsibility boundaries for
cross-market evidence, product identity, currency evidence,
regulatory evidence, shipping-route intelligence, landed-cost
intelligence, comparison, and recommendation consumption.

Review result:

```text
ARCHITECTURE CLASSIFICATION:

CONFIRMED
```

---

# 5. Selected Architecture Review

The governing selected architecture remains:

```text
Alternative C — Hybrid Commerce Intelligence
```

The formal architecture specification preserves the governing
decision that existing Commerce AI architecture should be reused
where responsibility already exists while dedicated cross-border
intelligence responsibilities should be introduced where the
existing architecture does not provide authoritative ownership.

This avoids both:

```text
1. unnecessary duplication of existing commerce intelligence

and

2. inappropriate expansion of existing components into
   cross-border responsibilities they do not canonically own
```

Review result:

```text
SELECTED ARCHITECTURE:

CONFIRMED
```

---

# 6. Component Completeness Review

CBC-FAS-2026-001 establishes the formal component structure required
to represent the accepted cross-border architecture.

The specification separates responsibilities rather than collapsing
them into a single cross-border service.

The formal component model provides architectural ownership for the
major cross-border intelligence concerns established by the
governing decision chain.

The review finds no architecture-level requirement to collapse these
responsibilities into fewer canonical authorities.

Review result:

```text
COMPONENT COMPLETENESS:

PASS
```

---

# 7. Authority Boundary Integrity Review

The formal architecture must preserve single-authority ownership for
canonical decisions and prevent downstream components from silently
becoming competing sources of truth.

The review confirms that the architecture distinguishes:

```text
evidence acquisition

from

canonical interpretation

from

derived intelligence

from

comparison

from

recommendation consumption
```

A component may consume authoritative evidence without becoming the
authority that owns that evidence.

A derived component may calculate a result without acquiring
authority over its upstream canonical inputs.

Recommendation logic may consume cross-border intelligence without
becoming the canonical owner of:

```text
product identity

currency evidence

regulatory evidence

shipping-route evidence

landed-cost evidence
```

Review result:

```text
AUTHORITY BOUNDARY INTEGRITY:

PASS
```

---

# 8. Dependency Direction Review

The architecture requires dependency direction to follow canonical
authority rather than convenience of implementation.

The expected architectural direction is:

```text
Authoritative Evidence
        ↓
Canonical Interpretation
        ↓
Derived Cross-Border Intelligence
        ↓
Cross-Market Comparison
        ↓
Recommendation Consumption
```

Reverse authority acquisition is prohibited.

In particular:

```text
Recommendation
must not become the authority for comparison evidence.

Comparison
must not become the authority for landed-cost inputs.

Landed Cost
must not become the authority for regulatory decisions.

Regulatory evaluation
must not invent missing source evidence.
```

Review result:

```text
DEPENDENCY DIRECTION:

PASS
```

---

# 9. Architecture Invariant Review

The formal architecture establishes invariants required to preserve
cross-border evidence integrity.

The review confirms the architectural requirement that unknown,
missing, stale, conflicting, or otherwise insufficient evidence must
not be silently converted into authoritative certainty.

The architecture must preserve the distinction between:

```text
known

unknown

unavailable

stale

conflicting

not applicable

not evaluated
```

where required by the canonical architecture decisions.

Derived outputs must not claim stronger evidence authority than their
inputs support.

Review result:

```text
ARCHITECTURE INVARIANTS:

PASS
```

---

# 10. Product Identity Boundary Review

Cross-market comparison depends on product identity but must not
assume that superficially similar listings represent the same
product.

The formal architecture preserves product identity as a canonical
boundary.

Comparison and recommendation consumers must distinguish, as
applicable:

```text
same product

equivalent product

alternative product

unresolved identity
```

The architecture therefore prevents price comparison from implicitly
becoming product-identity authority.

Review result:

```text
PRODUCT IDENTITY BOUNDARY:

PASS
```

---

# 11. Currency Evidence Boundary Review

Currency conversion is treated as evidence-dependent rather than as
an unqualified arithmetic constant.

The formal architecture preserves the requirement that currency
evidence carry sufficient context to support derived calculations.

Derived landed-cost or comparison results must not silently use
untraceable currency assumptions as authoritative evidence.

Review result:

```text
CURRENCY EVIDENCE BOUNDARY:

PASS
```

---

# 12. Regulatory Gate Review

The regulatory gate remains a required architecture responsibility.

Regulatory uncertainty must be represented explicitly.

The architecture does not permit an unknown regulatory state to be
silently converted into:

```text
allowed
```

or:

```text
prohibited
```

without authoritative evidence supporting that decision.

The regulatory gate is an intelligence and decision boundary within
the current architecture.

It is not authorization for customs filing or other transaction
execution.

Review result:

```text
REGULATORY GATE:

PASS
```

---

# 13. Shipping Route Review

Shipping-route intelligence is preserved as a distinct architectural
responsibility.

Route intelligence may represent candidate or evaluated logistics
paths but does not itself authorize operational shipment booking.

Shipping evidence must remain distinguishable from transaction
execution.

Review result:

```text
SHIPPING ROUTE ARCHITECTURE:

PASS
```

---

# 14. Landed-Cost Authority Review

The architecture establishes a canonical landed-cost responsibility
rather than permitting each consumer to independently invent its own
cross-border total-cost semantics.

Landed-cost calculation must consume authoritative upstream evidence
and preserve the evidence state of material inputs.

The architecture must not convert missing material cost inputs into
false precision.

Review result:

```text
LANDED-COST AUTHORITY:

PASS
```

---

# 15. Cross-Market Comparison Review

Cross-market comparison is a derived intelligence responsibility.

It consumes authoritative identity, market, currency, regulatory,
shipping, and landed-cost evidence as applicable.

It does not become the canonical authority for those upstream
domains.

Comparison outputs must preserve unresolved states where the
underlying evidence is insufficient for an authoritative comparison.

Review result:

```text
CROSS-MARKET COMPARISON:

PASS
```

---

# 16. Recommendation Ownership Review

The existing recommendation architecture remains the consumer-facing
recommendation authority.

Cross-border architecture supplies governed cross-border
intelligence to recommendation logic.

It does not create a competing recommendation authority.

This preserves existing architecture reuse while allowing
cross-border intelligence to extend the evidence available to the
recommendation system.

Review result:

```text
RECOMMENDATION OWNERSHIP:

PRESERVED

EXISTING ARCHITECTURE REUSE:

PASS
```

# 17. Evidence Provenance Review

Evidence provenance is a mandatory architecture concern.

The formal architecture preserves the requirement that evidence and
derived decisions remain traceable to their authoritative source or
governed provenance record.

The review confirms that provenance must not be discarded merely
because a downstream component derives a normalized or aggregated
result.

Review result:

```text
EVIDENCE PROVENANCE ARCHITECTURE:

PASS
```

# 18. Unknown-State Preservation Review

Unknown-state preservation is a required architecture invariant.

The system must not use fabricated certainty as a substitute for
missing evidence.

Where the architecture cannot establish a supported state, the
result must remain appropriately unresolved.

Review result:

```text
UNKNOWN STATE PRESERVATION:

PASS
```

# 19. Freshness Semantics Review

The formal architecture distinguishes evidence freshness from
evidence truth.

Evidence may be structurally valid while no longer sufficiently
current for a particular decision.

Freshness semantics therefore belong to the evidence and authority
model rather than being treated as an incidental cache concern.

Review result:

```text
FRESHNESS SEMANTICS:

PASS
```
